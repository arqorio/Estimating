What 2.5 adds — the frame calculator in the field

  • Its own keypad on a phone — the phone keyboard for numbers has no dash, slash or foot mark, so 3-0 or 36 1/2 could not be typed. Tap a size and a keypad opens with digits, a foot key, every fraction from 1/16 to 7/8, ±1/16 nudges and Next to go to the next field; the numbers update as you type.
  • The answer always in sight — the masonry or rough opening, the door, the clear width and the face lap stay pinned at the top while you type.
  • Door · Hole · Frame — the inputs in three tabs, each showing what is set, instead of one long page; sizes one tap away (2'-4" to 4'-0", pairs to 6'-8", heights 6'-8", 7'-0", 8'-0").
  • Measure the hole at several points — width at the top, middle and bottom, height at each jamb and both diagonals. The frame is sized to the smallest, and a hole out of plumb, out of level or out of square is called out with how much.
  • Keep and share — Share the numbers sends them by text, email or any app on the phone; the last ten calculations are kept under Recent to load again.

What 2.4 adds — the phone

  • Home — opens first on a phone: the job, its total and how it splits, what needs a look (tap to go to it), six big buttons for the things done on site — add an opening, size a frame, find a door, plans, the quote PDF, save the job — how far the job is (hardware, checks, on a plan) and the latest openings.
  • Openings as cards — mark, size, type and hand, the rooms, the rating, the set and any issue, drawn a screenful at a time so long jobs scroll smoothly. Tap one to open it; it slides in.
  • Gestures — swipe across an opening's header for the next or previous door, swipe in from the left edge to close it.
  • Bottom bar — Home, Openings, a raised + to add a door, Calc, More. The top bar keeps the job name and puts search, file and export on icons.
  • Fits the screen — every page, wide tables scroll inside their card, two column layouts stack; the iPhone notch and home bar are kept clear; a phone held sideways keeps the phone layout.
  • Smoother — no tap delay, a press response on every button, and a change to an opening no longer redraws the whole list behind it: on a slow phone with 300 openings a change went from over a second to about a quarter of a second.

What 2.3 adds — finding and reading the door schedule

  • Found in seconds, in any size set — every sheet is searched now, not the first 40. The search starts with the PDF bookmarks and the back of the set, where the schedules are; the schedule opens as soon as it is found, on its first sheet, while the rest of the set is still being searched. Each page's text is read once and kept, so changing pages and reading more sheets is instant.
  • Every schedule sheet listed — the sheets with a door schedule show as buttons above the page (sheet number and doors on each). Read all reads the whole schedule across its sheets in one click.
  • The right table on the sheet — a sheet with the door schedule printed in two or three blocks side by side reads as one schedule. Window, room finish and other schedules, frame type legends and general notes beside it are left out, using the title over each table. Rows must carry a real door mark, so notes and totals do not become doors. Sheets plotted sideways are turned upright.
  • Excel workbooks — every tab is checked and the door schedule tab opens on its own; the others are a click away. Merged headings (DOOR over WIDTH, HEIGHT) are carried into each column.
  • A TYPE column with SGL / PR sets single or pair, and a hand written in the remarks is used when there is no hand column.

What 2.2 adds — from estimators on r/Estimators

  • Handing off the plan — on a floor plan, Hand the doors from the swings reads every door swing drawn on the sheet (the arc and the leaf) and sets the hand of each door pinned on it: LH or RH seen from the push side, LHR or RHR when the opening swings out. Pairs are recognised, and with the drawing scale set a leaf that measures differently from the schedule is pointed out. Clicking a door on its swing hands it as it is added.
  • Unit types and flips — for apartments, hotels and dorms where the doors are tagged once on the enlarged unit plan. In Count on plan › Units: read the tags on the enlarged plan, hand them from the swings, then This sheet is the enlarged plan. On each floor plan, Find "UNIT A" counts the labels — a label with FLIP, MIRROR or REV on it or under it counts as flipped — or click each unit and click a pin again to flip it. Put on the job writes one line per door for the units as drawn and one for the flipped units with the hand mirrored, multiplied out.
  • Hardware sets from the specification — Plans › Hardware sets reads every set in a spec PDF (Section 08 71 00): quantity, item, catalog number, finish and maker, and puts the doors the spec lists on their set. Electrified items — electric strikes, power transfers, power supplies, card readers, mag locks, operators, door position switches — are flagged, and Export › Electrified hardware lists them door by door for the low voltage and security trades.

A takeoff and quoting tool for doors, frames and hardware

Type each opening in once. The tool does the rest: it works out the sizes, draws the opening so you can check it against the plan, counts the material, prices it, and prints the schedule, the blueprints and the customer quote. Nothing to install and no internet needed.

Development build — not released Built by Orelio Martinez Chow. Approved for development by management and shared for internal review only. Do not send this file outside the company, and do not use it as the only record of a live bid until it is signed off.

Why it exists

One entry, not four

Today an opening gets written down on a takeoff pad, again on a door schedule, again on a hardware list, and again in the quote. Every retype is a chance to lose a jamb depth or a fire rating. Here it is typed once and everything downstream is generated.

The estimator stops doing arithmetic

Masonry and rough openings, frame overall sizes, anchors per jamb, hinge counts, leaf totals and extended pricing all calculate themselves as you type.

Mistakes surface before the order

The tool flags cold rolled steel on an exterior opening, a labeled door with no closer reinforcement, a wood door without a mineral core, masonry anchors in a stud wall, a lockset with no strike. These are the ones that come back as a shop error or a field change.

Quote-ready output

An eleven-sheet Excel workbook, a CSV, or a printed schedule — with the counts already grouped the way a purchase order needs them.

How the work flows

Step by step

1Job info

Fill in the job name, number, GC, architect, bid date and estimator. Set the markups you want on frames, doors, hardware and labor, plus your install rate.

These print on the schedule and drive every price in the recap.

2Openings

Press Add opening, or open the arrow beside it to start from a preset — "Exterior HM, galvannealed" fills about twenty fields at once. Type the size as 3070, 36x84 or 3-0 x 7-0.

The panel on the right draws the opening as you type and lists anything that looks wrong. Ten collapsible sections hold the full record: mark and location, size, scope, door, frame, preps, fire rating, handing, hardware and pricing.

For a run of openings, the quick entry row above the schedule takes a mark, size, type, preset, rating, hand, set and rooms and adds the line when you press Enter. For a whole schedule at once use Batch add & import: one line per opening, a different door and frame on each line, or paste the lines straight out of a spreadsheet. Tick several rows and use Change a field to set one value across all of them.

You get a live door schedule, a dimensioned elevation, and a running conflict list.

3Hardware

Build each set the way it reads on the drawings: a set number, a group, a name, then the items — product name, model, manufacturer, BHMA finish, function, keying, size and cost.

You do not have to start from scratch. Every opening shows the hardware it actually needs, worked out from its preps, rating, swing and location, and one button builds the set from that list using the product library.

Set costs roll into every opening carrying that set number.

4Takeoff sheet

The complete count: every opening on one wide line, then doors grouped by identical spec, frames grouped by profile and depth, hardware extended across the openings that use it, and glass and louvers.

This is the page you order and quote from.

5Recap

Totals by category, the production split between stock and custom, gross margin, and a list of any opening still holding a conflict.

Export to Excel or CSV, or print it.

AAbbreviations

Every code the tool writes and where it comes from — SDI, ANSI, BHMA, NFPA 80 and WDMA. Useful for anyone new to hollow metal, and for settling an argument about what a jamb depth is.

Ships in the Excel export too, so the customer gets the legend.

Reading the drawing

Elevation

The frame, header and leaves at scale, with the wall hatched behind. Hinges sit at their standard locations, the strike centerline is marked at 40-5/16 inches above the floor, closer reinforcement shows in the head, and vision lites, louvers and the undercut appear where you specify them.

Dimension lines below give the nominal opening and the rough or masonry opening; the left side gives the opening height and the frame overall height.

Jamb section

The same opening cut through the strike jamb: the face, the door rabbet, the stop, the back rabbet and the throat against the wall, each labeled. It is the quickest way to confirm a jamb depth is right for the wall before the frame is ordered.

Coming from the current takeoff form

Nothing about the ordering changes

The tool speaks the same Curries vocabulary the May 2023 workbook does. The Legend code table, the jamb depth codes from 3 in to 12 in in eighths, the hinge, strike, lock and closer prep codes, the anchor systems, the face types, the handing codes and the manufacturer locations are all in here.

Every opening produces the same two strings the form produces, in the same format:

5-3/4" 3068 RH WELDED FRAME | WIRE.MAS | 3-HNG 1-478.STK 90 MIN
HM 3068 RH DOOR  | FLUSH | 3-HNG CYL.161 CURRIES 90 MIN

Purchasing gets exactly what it gets today, with the character count beside it.

The codes fill themselves in

On the form, the estimator picks a code in every column and the description is assembled by formula. Here it runs the other way: describe the opening in plain terms once, and the codes are derived — welded frame becomes WGS, a wire masonry anchor becomes WMA, three hinges become U453, a 161 cylindrical lock becomes G2 and the frame strike becomes E1.

Every code is still a dropdown set to AUTO, so any of them can be overridden on any opening when the job calls for something different.

Fewer picks per opening, and the codes stay internally consistent.

Product library and blueprint sheets

The frame and door pickers hold the items that go out most often — Curries and BSC (Baron) hollow metal, plus wood — and fill the whole spec and the cost in one pick. The hardware library carries Hager, Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN, Rockwood, Ives, NGP and Pemko products.

The elevation draws transoms and sidelites as well as the door and frame, and toggles between the whole opening, the frame on its own, the door on its own and the jamb depth section.

The two elevation exports under Export write tabloid sheets, 17 x 11 landscape with a title block: one set for frames and one for doors, elevations at a stated architectural scale followed by the full specification schedule and the general notes.

Anchors are scheduled as individual items — jamb, base, head, sidelite, spreaders and silencers each counted on their own line, never rolled into the frame.

Quotation the customer can actually read

The Quotation step turns the takeoff into a customer facing price. Every opening is described in plain words — "single door, 3 ft 0 in wide by 7 ft 0 in tall, hollow metal steel door, 16 gauge, with a polyurethane insulated core, in a welded one piece frame built for a concrete block wall" — with no trade shorthand at all. Identical openings are grouped into one priced line.

Four comment boxes print straight onto it: what the price includes, what it does not include, notes for the customer, and terms. A fifth box holds internal notes that appear only on the bid recap.

Two clean letter sized PDFs: the quotation for the customer and the bid recap for the estimator, with an acceptance signature block on the quotation.

Project catalog

A single list of everything going into the job — frames, doors, every hardware item, glass, louvers, anchors — with the openings each one belongs to and a column for the question you need answered. Mark a line CONFIRM or QUESTION, type the question, and send the PDF to the client or the architect as it stands.

Press Rebuild from the takeoff whenever the job changes. It pulls the list again and keeps everything you have written in the status and question columns.

Also exports to CSV, and you can add lines by hand for anything not on the takeoff.

Import what already exists

Batch add has an importer. Select the data rows on the FRAME & DOOR, FRAME ONLY or DOOR ONLY sheet, copy them, and paste. The codes are translated back into full entries, so an imported job immediately draws, checks and prices like anything typed in directly.

Jobs in progress do not have to be re-entered.

Three sheets become one list

The workbook keeps FRAME & DOOR, FRAME ONLY and DOOR ONLY as three separate sheets with three copies of the same formulas. Here it is one list, and each opening is routed to the right group automatically from its frame and door scope — the takeoff sheet still prints them grouped that way.

One place to fix a formula, not three.

Practical notes

Everything lives in one file. The tool is a single HTML file. Open it in any browser — Chrome, Edge, Safari — on a desktop, laptop or tablet. There is nothing to install, no account, no server and no internet connection required. Work is kept in the browser as you go; File › Save job file writes a job file you can put on the shared drive or email, and File › Open job file loads it back. One file per job keeps the history clean.

Questions people ask

Do the prices in it come from anywhere?

No. Frame, door and hardware costs are typed in, and the starter hardware sets carry placeholder numbers so the math is visible. Replace them with your buy prices once and they carry forward on every job. Connecting it to a live price list is the obvious next step.

What if the drawings call for something the tool does not list?

Every dropdown has a custom or special option, every section has a notes field, and any opening can be forced to a custom elevation. Nothing is locked.

Does it replace the shop drawing?

No. The elevation and jamb section are there so the estimator can check the takeoff against the plan before it goes out. Custom openings still get a real shop drawing.

Can two people work on the same job?

Not at the same time. It is a file, so it passes between people the way a spreadsheet does. Shared, simultaneous editing would need a hosted version.

What happens to the data?

It stays on the machine it was entered on and in the job file you save. Nothing is transmitted anywhere.

How long does an opening take to enter?

With a preset, under a minute for a typical one. A schedule of repeated openings goes in through Batch add in a few minutes for the whole floor.
